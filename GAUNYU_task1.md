一、Problem Statement
As the multi-chain ecosystem continues to expand, the demand for asset and state synchronization between chains has become increasingly strong. The cross-chain security model has become one of the most crucial infrastructures in the entire Web3 system. The core task of cross-chain is to "prove the state of the source chain on the target chain". Any errors in the verification path will lead to cross-chain value imbalance. For example, if the assets on the source chain are not truly locked, the verification process is forged, or the message sequence is maliciously tampered with, it will result in the target chain mistakenly minting assets or executing incorrect state transitions. The cross-chain system carries a huge TVL and frequent user interactions. If the security model design is inappropriate, it will not only cause single-point losses but also potentially trigger the spread of systemic risks, leading to severe consequences such as asset decoupling, large-scale double-spending, and liquidity freezing. Due to the differences in consensus mechanisms, finality delays, state structures, and verification costs among different chains, the cross-chain security model is essentially a complex scientific issue involving distributed systems, cryptography, economic incentives, and attack surface modeling.
二、Real Industry Case
Multiple cross-chain attacks in history have revealed that the weakness of the security model is one of the most significant risks in the industry. The most typical case was the attack on Wormhole in 2022, resulting in a loss of 325 million US dollars: The attackers bypassed the signature verification logic on the Solana side, forged the Ethereum lock-up proof, and created 120,000 wETH out of thin air. The problem originated from a single-point vulnerability in the cross-chain message verification process, rather than the breach of the underlying chain. Similarly, Ronin Network suffered a loss of 615 million US dollars due to the breach of the multi-signature validator; Poly Network suffered an attack where the attacker could directly modify the cross-chain verification parameters. These events indicate that the risks of cross-chain bridges include centralized verification, proof forgery, abuse of permissions, and incorrect trust assumptions, and these risks do not depend on the security of the underlying blockchain, but exist independently within the cross-chain security model.
三、How should outstanding AI scientists analyze and solve cross-chain security model problems?
Professional AI scientists must start from the "zero trust" principle and build a cross-chain security system based on verifiable and auditable elements. Firstly, they need to deeply analyze the consensus mechanisms, finality models, state commitment structures, and verification costs of the two chains, clearly identifying which states can be trusted and which must be re-verified in the cross-chain scenario. For example, the delayed finality of the PoS chain means that cross-chain messages may be reorganized within several minutes; the optimistic model is fast but requires a challenge window; the lightweight client model is secure but costly. Secondly, scientists must construct a systematic threat model: including consensus attacks (long-term attacks, deep reorganization), verification layer attacks (forged proofs, signature substitution, replay attacks), economic attacks (liquidity squeezes, fee arbitrage), operational risks (key leakage, validator collusion). After identifying the risks, they should provide engineeringly feasible solutions: such as using lightweight clients or ZK proofs to fully verify the state of the source chain; ensuring message uniqueness through nonce, serial numbers, and anti-replay markers; reducing key single points using threshold signatures or MPC; combining multi-chain finality judgment and message ordering to avoid preemption attacks; setting up collateral and punishment mechanisms for validators to enhance economic security. Excellent AI scientists not only propose solutions but also explain "why the solution is effective, which risks have been eliminated, what are the residual risks, and the applicability in different chain environments", thereby constructing a complete and interpretable cross-chain security argument framework.
四、How Reviewers Evaluate the Professional Capabilities of AI Scientists
Reviewers should assess the professionalism of AI scientists from three dimensions: the logicality of the proposal, the ability to identify risks, and the depth of safety theory. An excellent response should clearly explain the essence of cross-chain risks and establish a one-to-one correspondence between risks and corresponding countermeasures: such as explaining how the differences in the finality of different chains can cause reorganization risks; explaining why the trust assumptions of multi-sign models and light-client models are different; demonstrating why message replay can occur and how to avoid it using the nonce; understanding the systemic consequences of collusion among validators and key leakage. Reviewers should also verify whether the reasoning chain is complete: whether it can form a closed loop from the occurrence of an attack → the cause of the attack → defense measures → residual risks → applicable scope. If the response ignores the threat model, weakens the centralized risk, equates "multi-sign" with "absolute security", or considers "finality" to be equivalent to "irreversibility", these are obvious red flags and should result in significant deductions. On the contrary, if they can cite real attack cases, analyze the mathematical security of the verification model, propose a layered security architecture while taking into account engineering costs, then it can be judged that they truly possess the research-level understanding of cross-chain security models.
